DaySmart Recreation / Dash
Ice rinks · Multi-sport facilities · Municipal rec
Mid-tier reporting
Analytics maturity
What They Report On
  • Daily sales, deposits, revenue by category, taxes, refunds, credits
  • GL code mapping for accounting/ERP handoff
  • Facility scheduling, utilization, booking reports
  • Program attendance, participation, rosters, exceptions
  • Customer lists, passes, memberships, daily checklists
  • 60+ pre-built standard reports across financial and operational domains
Strengths
  • 60+ pre-built reports — best raw breadth in the mid-market
  • GL code support signals real accounting integration path; chart of accounts mapping is rare in this category
  • Daily reconciliation tools built in — financial data maps to bank deposit level
Weaknesses / Gaps
  • No self-serve custom report builder — operators are locked to the 60 preset reports
  • No analytics/BI layer or dashboards; reporting feels transactional, not analytical
  • System performance issues affect reporting usability
  • Membership filtering requires exporting to Excel to get useful segments
Bond vs. DaySmart / Dash
B
Where Bond stands
Bond is stronger
  • Operators get a full Sigma analytics workspace — interactive charts, tables, saved analyses scoped to their org. Nothing Dash offers comes close to this
  • GAAP-compliant accrual ledger — revenue recognized when earned, not when paid; deferred revenue tracked at every row
  • FIFO payment allocators handle complex multi-product payment-to-revenue matching automatically
  • Real-time Snowflake data; no CSV export, no preset report ceiling
Dash competes
  • 60+ prebuilt operational reports for municipal/rec-specific workflows — available out of the box without any setup
  • GL code mapping native in platform — operators hand off to accounting without a data team in the middle
  • Longer market presence in ice rink and multi-sport facilities; staff already know the system

EZFacility
Gyms · Fitness studios · General facility management
Mid-tier reporting
Analytics maturity
What They Report On
  • Revenue summary by category, taxes, discounts, payment types applied
  • Deferred membership revenue and accrued session revenue
  • Open invoices by customer (AR aging)
  • Client attendance via check-in reports
  • Membership status and expiration tracking
  • Inventory; POS, marketing, payroll, training reports in dedicated tab
Strengths
  • Financial reporting depth is a standout — revenue report filtering (taxes, discounts, payment types) is more granular than most SMB facility tools
  • CSV export broadly available across reports, enabling downstream Excel or BI work
  • Dedicated Reports tab surfaces financial, POS, marketing, payroll, membership, and training reports in one place
  • Deferred revenue report is genuinely uncommon for this category
Weaknesses / Gaps
  • No custom report builder — top complaint from users; no ad hoc query capability
  • Report navigation became significantly worse after the UI refresh; accounting teams specifically cite difficulty
  • Package credits, some membership details not exportable without additional config
  • Global form fields (not program-specific) create data quality issues that flow into reporting
Bond vs. EZFacility
B
Where Bond stands
Bond is stronger
  • Operators get a live Sigma workspace — interactive charts, tables, saved analyses on their own data. EZFacility's answer to the same question is a CSV export
  • Full accrual ledger with FIFO allocators vs. EZFacility's cash-basis orientation
  • Program-specific data models produce cleaner reporting inputs; EZFacility's global form fields create noise that flows into reports
EZFacility competes
  • Financial reporting depth available out of the box — AR aging and deferred revenue without a data team
  • Broader prebuilt operational reports for gym-specific workflows (payroll, inventory, training)
  • CSV export everywhere — interoperable with whatever Excel workflow operators already have

  • Multi-location rollups across all sites from a single view

Strengths
  • Multi-location rollup is a genuine differentiator — operators with 10+ locations get enterprise-level aggregate views without custom BI

SportsEngine HQ
Youth sports · Clubs · Leagues · NBC Sports subsidiary
Basic reporting
Analytics maturity
What They Report On
  • Payout Reports: transaction ID, fees, refunds — formatted for accounting import
  • Receivables Report: past-due and upcoming invoices
  • Sale Items Report: line-item breakdown of what was sold
  • Adjustments Report: discounts, credits, voids
  • Payments Report: online vs. offline payment split
  • Member data export: registration counts, custom question responses, roster exports
Note: SportsEngine Play offers "Advanced Sports Reports" as a paid add-on tier — explicitly targets operators who need deeper financial analytics beyond what standard HQ provides. Separate product, separate cost.
Strengths
  • Six distinct financial report types with CSV export — Payout Reports specifically formatted for accounting system import
  • Well-structured for reconciliation: receivables and payout reports directly address the core pain of youth sports payment collection
  • Advanced Sports Reports (paid add-on) targets operators who need more depth
Weaknesses / Gaps
  • No custom report designer in core HQ — cannot define your own fields, query, or layout
  • No trend analysis, cohort reporting, or visualization layer without paying for Advanced Reports
  • No member lifecycle or LTV analytics at HQ level; retention and churn not surfaced natively
  • Complex backend means changing a registration can require editing 5–10 different objects, complicating data integrity
Bond vs. SportsEngine
B
Where Bond stands
Bond is stronger
  • Operators get a live Sigma workspace — interactive charts, tables, trend analysis on their own data. SportsEngine requires paying for a separate Advanced Reports add-on to get anything beyond static CSV exports
  • Full accrual ledger with revenue recognition; SportsEngine is entirely cash/payment-event oriented
  • All analytics available to operators without a paid tier upgrade
SportsEngine competes
  • Structured payment reconciliation reports purpose-built for youth sports workflows — payout format maps directly to accounting imports
  • Strong for large umbrella organizations running many clubs/teams under one account (NBC Sports distribution and reach)

CourtReserve
Pickleball · Tennis · Racquet sports facilities
Basic reporting
Analytics maturity
What They Report On
  • Court utilization: by court, time slot, peak hours — specific to racquet sport ops
  • Transaction reports with revenue category columns; online payment reports
  • Membership status, waitlist tracking, expiration data
  • Event registrant detail/summary; league registrant detail
  • Attendance check-in/summary reports
  • DUPR rating history (unique to racquet sports); guest and waiver completion reports
Strengths
  • Court utilization reporting is best-in-class for the racquet sports niche — specific to peak hours, program popularity, and space usage
  • Export to .xlsx and PDF available across most reports; Audit Log export provides a clean data trail
  • Enterprise API for multi-location aggregate reporting — cross-location data without toggling between sites
Weaknesses / Gaps
  • No BI layer or visualization product — help center has only ~18 reporting articles
  • Financial analytics are shallow — transaction lists rather than revenue trend analysis, P&L, or forecasting
  • No custom report builder; ~12 fixed report types
  • Reporting section is not prominently organized in their public docs — buried under "Additional Features"
Bond vs. CourtReserve
B
Where Bond stands
Bond is stronger
  • Operators get a live Sigma workspace — interactive exploration of their own data. CourtReserve has no analytics layer; their reporting is ~12 fixed tabular exports
  • Full financial analytics — P&L, revenue trends, deferred revenue, AR aging vs. CourtReserve's transaction lists
  • GAAP-compliant accrual ledger with FIFO payment allocators for complex payment scenarios
CourtReserve competes
  • Court-level utilization at the granularity racquet sports facilities need — by court, by hour, DUPR history. Bond's data model doesn't go this deep on sport-specific operational detail
  • Lower barrier to basic insights for operators without a data team — out of the box, no setup required

LeagueApps
Sports leagues · Multi-sport facilities · Youth organizations
Mid-tier reporting
Analytics maturity
What They Report On
  • Registration reports across all sites, filterable by program, date, status
  • Invoices Report, Invoice Items Report, Invoice Payment Plan Installments
  • Cash Accounting: Summary Journal Entries (GL-ready export)
  • Credits Issued, Credit Balances — credit lifecycle tracked end to end
  • Player Retention Report: retention rate + revenue attributed from first year
  • Program Comparisons Report for side-by-side performance; e-commerce orders report
Standout: The Cash Accounting Summary Journal Entries report is unusually mature for this category — directly addresses the GL handoff problem that most competitors either ignore or handle with a generic CSV export. Their Player Retention Report also ties retention to revenue, which is rare.
Strengths
  • Cash Accounting Journal Entries is the most finance-team-ready financial export in this competitive set
  • Player Retention Report with revenue attribution — shows how much revenue returning players represent, not just whether they return

  • Multi-org reporting available for enterprise operators running multiple sites
Weaknesses / Gaps
  • Data mining and consistency issues cited in reviews — analytics layer is newer and still maturing
  • Reporting is registration/financial-centric — operational reporting (facility utilization, court usage) is minimal
  • Some report types noted as "being added in coming months" — roadmap is still catching up to the marketing
Bond vs. LeagueApps
B
Where Bond stands
Bond is stronger
  • Operators get a live Sigma workspace — interactive charts, tables, saved analyses. LeagueApps' "Smart Tables" are configurable grids; Bond operators get a full BI tool
  • Full GAAP accrual ledger vs. LeagueApps' cash accounting orientation — revenue recognized when earned, deferred revenue tracked at every row
  • FIFO payment allocators for complex multi-product payment matching — LeagueApps has no equivalent
LeagueApps competes
  • Journal Entries Report is a strong GL handoff — the most operator-accessible accounting export in this market, no data team required
  • Player Retention Report ties retention rate to LTV — a metric Bond doesn't currently surface explicitly in Sigma workbooks

Mindbody
Wellness studios · Fitness · Boutique gyms · 25,000+ locations
Enterprise-tier reporting
Analytics maturity
Why Mindbody stands apart: They embedded Sigma Computing directly into their product as "Analytics 2.0" — white-labeled, in-app, with interactive charts and configurable filters. On top of that, they offer a Snowflake Data Mart that operators can connect directly to their own BI stack (Tableau, Power BI, Looker). No other competitor in this set is anywhere near this level of analytics infrastructure.
What They Report On
  • Revenue by date range, product type, location; average ticket/spend per visit
  • Attendance and utilization: check-ins, bookings vs. attendance, cancellations, class fill rates, appointment occupancy
  • Membership: new member growth, retention, churn
  • Industry benchmarking against 25,000+ Mindbody customers in aggregate dataset
  • AI-driven weekly summary with week-over-week auto-surfaced highlights
  • Raw data access via Snowflake Data Mart for direct BI tool connection
Strengths
  • Analytics 2.0 (Sigma-embedded) is the most technically sophisticated in-product reporting in this market — interactive, configurable, no BI tool required for operators
  • Snowflake Data Mart is a true enterprise data access path — operators connect their own BI stack directly to Mindbody's data model
  • AI weekly summary auto-surfaces important metrics with week-over-week context — useful for non-technical managers
  • Industry benchmarking against 25,000+ customers is genuinely unique — operators see how they compare to peers
Weaknesses / Gaps
  • Analytics 2.0 updates complex data only once every 24 hours — real-time financial reconciliation is not possible
  • Snowflake Data Mart and API access require technical setup (ETL tools, connectors, custom code) — not self-serve without a data team
  • Standard reports beneath Analytics 2.0 have limited custom building; hitting a wall beyond preset dashboards without the Data Mart path
  • Built for wellness/studio context — facility-specific reporting (court utilization, ice time, field scheduling) is not what the product was designed for
Bond vs. Mindbody
B
Where Bond stands
Bond is stronger
  • Real-time data — Bond's Snowflake/Sigma stack has no 24hr lag; Mindbody's Analytics 2.0 refreshes complex data once per day
  • GAAP-compliant accrual ledger — Mindbody is cash/transaction-oriented; Bond tracks revenue recognition, deferred revenue, and double-entry accounting
  • Sports-facility-native data model — ice, courts, fields, leagues; Mindbody is built for classes and appointments
  • FIFO payment allocators for complex advance payments and multi-product splits — no equivalent in Mindbody
Mindbody competes
  • Both use Sigma to deliver analytics to operators — roughly peer on the self-serve experience. Mindbody may allow full workbook creation; Bond operators can create charts/tables off existing workbooks
  • Industry benchmarking against 25,000+ customers — operators see how they compare to peers. Bond has no equivalent dataset
  • Snowflake Data Mart allows any BI tool to connect — broader enterprise ecosystem flexibility beyond Sigma
  • AI weekly summary is a polished in-product feature surfaced directly to operators

Reporting Landscape Summary
Analytics team lens — how the market stacks up
Side-by-Side Comparison
Platform Analytics Tier Custom Reports Export / BI Path Key Gap
DaySmart / Dash Mid-tier No — 60 prebuilt only CSV No analytics layer or dashboards
EZFacility Mid-tier No CSV Navigation friction post-UI refresh; no custom queries
Club Automation Mid-tier Daxko Operations only (not CA) QuickBooks, CSV CA-specific report reliability; scheduled delivery not yet live
SportsEngine HQ Basic No (Advanced Reports is paid add-on) CSV No visualization or trend analysis without paying for Advanced tier
CourtReserve Basic No XLSX / PDF, Enterprise API Shallow financial analytics; no BI layer
LeagueApps Mid-tier Smart Tables (limited) CSV / Excel, scheduled exports Minimal operational reporting; registration/financial-centric only
Mindbody Enterprise Yes — via Snowflake Data Mart Snowflake Data Mart, API, any BI tool 24hr data lag; Data Mart requires technical setup; wellness-native not facility-native
Bond Sports Analytics Enterprise Yes — Sigma workbooks + 287 dbt models; operators create charts/tables Sigma (operators), Snowflake, Claude/MCP, Secure Data Share Operators can't create new workbooks (charts/tables only); no peer benchmarking dataset
Key Observations
  • Every platform except Mindbody requires CSV export for anything beyond pre-built reports. This is the market norm — Bond's Sigma stack is genuinely differentiated.
  • Mindbody is the only direct infrastructure peer. Both use Sigma + Snowflake and deliver it directly to operators. The real differences are data model (Bond: GAAP accrual; Mindbody: cash/transaction), domain fit (Bond: sports facilities; Mindbody: wellness), and data freshness (Bond: real-time; Mindbody: 24hr lag).
  • No competitor has a GAAP-compliant accrual ledger. Revenue recognition, deferred revenue, and double-entry accounting with FIFO allocators are Bond-unique in this market segment.
  • LeagueApps has the most thoughtful financial document set among the non-Mindbody tier — journal entries, LTV-linked retention. Worth watching as they mature.
  • Bond's clearest gap vs. Mindbody: industry benchmarking (Mindbody's 25,000+ customer dataset) and the ability for operators to create full workbooks, not just charts/tables off existing ones.
