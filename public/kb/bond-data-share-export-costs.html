Bond Snowflake Data Share: Setup & Cost Guide

How to get your Bond data flowing into your own analytics stack via Snowflake — and what (if anything) it'll cost you. Two scenarios are covered: (1) you already have Snowflake, or (2) you need a daily copy of your data in another platform.

What this is

Bond publishes your organization's data as a live, read-only Snowflake share. Once enabled, 12 views in the ANALYTICS_SHARED schema appear as a database inside your Snowflake account and refresh automatically as your data updates — typically within minutes. There's no ETL job to build, no daily file to manage, and no extra fee from Bond.

Bond's Snowflake account is hosted on AWS US East (Ohio) — region code us-east-2. This only matters in one specific case: if you're unloading the data to your own cloud storage (S3/GCS/Azure Blob) on the way to a non-Snowflake platform, a same-region bucket avoids egress fees and a cross-region one adds a small one.

How to enable the share

The setup is the same regardless of which row above describes you — the share is the entry point in both cases. Your Bond CSM handles our side; this section covers what we need from you and what you'll run on yours.

1. Make sure you have a Snowflake account

Any Snowflake edition works — Standard is the entry-level on consumption pricing and is more than enough for accessing the share. If you don't have a Snowflake account yet, sign up at signup.snowflake.com. Choose AWS, and if you're creating an account specifically to access Bond data, pick the us-east-2 (Ohio) region to keep things simple.

2. Send your CSM two pieces of information

  • Your Snowflake account identifier in ORG_NAME.ACCOUNT_NAME form
  • Your Snowflake region (e.g., AWS_US_EAST_2)

You can find both by running this in your Snowflake account:

SELECT
  CURRENT_ORGANIZATION_NAME() || '.' || CURRENT_ACCOUNT_NAME() AS account_identifier,
  CURRENT_REGION() AS region;

Your CSM will reply confirming your account has been added to the share. You can then move on to step 3 — no further info needed from us.

3. Create the database from the listing

Bond's listing ID is GZT1Z25AYVC. Run this once in your Snowflake account:

SHOW AVAILABLE LISTINGS;
CREATE DATABASE bond_data FROM LISTING 'GZT1Z25AYVC';

You can name the database whatever you like — bond_data is just a suggestion.

4. Query the share to confirm it's working

The shared views live in the ANALYTICS_SHARED schema. Each view is automatically filtered to your organization — no need to add WHERE organization_id = filters.

SELECT * FROM bond_data.ANALYTICS_SHARED.CUSTOMERS LIMIT 10;

5. Grant access to your team

GRANT IMPORTED PRIVILEGES ON DATABASE bond_data TO ROLE your_analytics_role;

The share refreshes automatically as Bond's data updates — typically within minutes.

If you see "Shared data not available" right after creating the database (especially if your Snowflake account is in a different region from ours), give it 10–30 minutes for replication to complete. If the error persists, reach out to your Bond CSM and we'll work with Snowflake support to resolve.
If you're already on Snowflake, you can stop reading here. The data is already "in" your warehouse the moment your CSM grants the share. There's no ETL, no movement, no daily job, no extra fee — even if your Snowflake account is on a different cloud or region than ours. The rest of this guide covers the second scenario only: moving a copy to a non-Snowflake platform.

The short answer on cost (for the "moving to another platform" case)

For most organizations, a full daily export of your Bond data into another platform costs a few cents per day in Snowflake compute — typically $1–$30 per month, depending on your size and how you run it. There are no storage charges on shared data, and no Bond-side fees.

You're paying for two things only:

  • Compute (credits) — every time you query or unload from the share, you spin up a virtual warehouse. Snowflake bills per second with a 60-second minimum each time the warehouse resumes.
  • Egress — only if the destination is in a different cloud region from Bond's (us-east-2). Same-region unloads are free.

You do not pay for storage of the shared tables (Bond pays this), a Snowflake Enterprise tier (Standard On-Demand is enough), or anything to Bond — the share itself is included with your Bond plan.

How much data is "all of it"?

The 12 shared tables grow roughly in proportion to your customer count and transactional activity. Here's what a daily full export looks like across three reference sizes:

Org sizeCustomersRows / dayUncompressedCompressed
Small — single facility~5,000~360,000~580 MB~80–130 MB
Medium — established multi-program~20,000~1.5 million~2.5 GB~350–500 MB
Large — multi-facility enterprise~75,000~3.7 million~5.7 GB~800 MB–1.2 GB

By a wide margin, the biggest contributor is ACTIONS — a transactional ledger that records every revenue, payment, and deferred-revenue event for your organization (used for financial reconciliation and reporting). It accounts for roughly two-thirds of the total volume at every size. Because it's transactional, pulling only new and changed rows each day shrinks it dramatically — see Tips below. Organizations with heavy membership billing or payment-plan-heavy programs can run 3–5× larger than the averages above.

Three ways to run it

Pick one approach. Your monthly cost depends on which one you use and how much data your organization generates.

  • Same-region unload — a scheduled COPY INTO @stage drops CSV or Parquet files into your S3, GCS, or Azure Blob bucket, in the same cloud region as Bond's Snowflake account.
  • Cross-region unload — same idea, but the destination is in a different cloud or region (adds a small egress fee).
  • JDBC / ODBC connector — an off-the-shelf tool (Fivetran-style, a BI tool's native connector, a custom script) pulls rows over the wire. Easiest to set up; warehouse stays running longer.

At the extreme high end — roughly 20 GB/day on ACTIONS alone — plan for ~$25–50/month on same-region unloads, or reach out to your Bond CSM for a tailored estimate.

Tips to keep your bill minimal

  • Use a single X-Small warehouse for daily exports — anything bigger is wasted on this volume.
  • Run all tables in one session so you pay the 60-second warehouse minimum once, not twelve times.
  • Set auto-suspend to 60 seconds on the export warehouse.
  • Go incremental on ACTIONS — filter on ACTION_TIME >= DATEADD(day, -1, CURRENT_DATE()). ACTIONS is two-thirds of your volume; pulling only new and changed rows can cut total daily bytes by 5–10× and meaningfully reduce medium- and large-tier compute. For the other 11 tables, filter on _UPDATED_AT.
  • Co-locate your Snowflake account in the same cloud region as Bond's if you have a choice — eliminates egress entirely.

What this guide does not cover

  • Costs of the destination platform (your warehouse, BI tool, or third-party connector vendor)
  • Ad-hoc analytics workloads — those are typically much larger than a daily export, and cost depends on how heavily your team queries the data
One thing to double-check: Snowflake's per-credit pricing varies slightly by cloud, region, and edition. The numbers in this guide assume Standard Edition On-Demand on AWS US East (Ohio) at $2.00/credit. Your effective rate may be a few cents higher or lower.
